# Register exemplars and contrast pairs (§7.2 quantities 1 and 2)

Status: PROVISIONAL SEEDS. The owner's hand-written exemplars are the eval set
(PLAN.md §7.2: "the owner's asymmetry — can write the target, cannot explain
it — is the project's key resource"). What ships here is enough structure to
run the harness end to end and to shake it down against the Milestone 1
checkpoints; every entry is marked seed and must be replaced by owner writing
before any ablation is scored on it. Editing this file is the entire
interface: add a `## name` section, done. The contamination guard runs over
whatever is here.

Format: `## <unique-id>` sections, prose body, `---` terminated. A section
whose id contains `.contrast` is read as the contrast member of the pair
named by stripping `.contrast`. Pair members must be roughly
length-matched (the loss gap is per-token, so a 2x length difference
contaminates the gap with a length effect; the harness reports both members'
perplexities so an unbalanced pair is visible in the output, not hidden).

What a pair IS (RL_STRATEGY §3): target writing vs. superficially-similar-
but-wrong writing — wrong in the way one can feel but not articulate. The
wrong member imitates the surface (period diction, formal cadence, archaic
vocabulary) while missing whatever the right member has. The gap between
their losses under the model should WIDEN as the mix works. If the seeds below
are too easy to separate, that is fine for shake-down and wrong for science;
the owner's pairs replace them.

## seed_cold_open_1 (seed — replace with owner writing)

The house had been empty since the winter of the year the canal froze, and it
had not been emptied by anyone. The rooms had simply tired of being watched,
one after another, and had closed their eyes. What furniture remained stood
where the last family had set it, but the chairs had turned their backs to the
windows, all of them, in a manner no one remembered and no one would have
chosen. Dust lay on the sills in the shape of small hands. In the kitchen the
water in the kettle had gone on boiling long after the fire was out, which the
neighbours could hear at night, a thin sound like a kettle remembering, and
they crossed themselves when they passed the gate.

## seed_cold_open_1.contrast (seed — replace with owner writing)

The house had been empty since the winter of the year the canal froze, and it
had not been emptied by anyone. The rooms remained much as the last family had
left them, and the furniture stood in its ordinary places. Dust had gathered
on the window sills over the course of several seasons, as is usual in a
dwelling that no one occupies. In the kitchen the kettle had been left upon
the stove, and the neighbours passing the gate at night remarked among
themselves that the property was a great pity, and that someone ought to see
to it.

## seed_mundane_1 (seed — replace with owner writing)

An account of how a kitchen is arranged. The kitchen arranges itself around
the stove, as a parish arranges itself around its church, and every other
object in the room takes its station according to the ceremony of heat. The
stove is first, and the hearth is its older sister, retired but still
consulted. The pots hang in order of seniority. The dresser holds the plates
the way a vault holds its dead, faces down, quiet, each in its felt-lined
slot. The window is placed so the morning comes in across the sink and the
water in the basin is the first thing in the house to know the day has begun.
Bread is kept in the tin with the fallen lid, which keeps the bread from
hearing the news, and so it keeps longer. The floor is scrubbed on Fridays so
the house has one clean hour before Sunday comes and everyone forgets
themselves again.

## seed_mundane_1.contrast (seed — replace with owner writing)

An account of how a kitchen is arranged. The kitchen is arranged around the
stove, which supplies heat for the cooking of meals. Near the stove are placed
the pots and pans, which are used for the preparation of food. The dresser
contains the plates and dishes, kept in their proper order for daily use. At
the window is the sink, where the washing up is done, and the bread is kept in
a tin to protect it from the air. The floor is swept daily and scrubbed at the
week's end, so that the kitchen remains in a clean and serviceable condition
for the household.
